# Organized Crime Drug Enforcement Task Forces (OCDETF)

Edition label: `2026-10`

**Definition:** A federal multi-agency task-force program established during the Reagan administration (1982) to coordinate major-scale investigations targeting transnational organized crime, drug trafficking, and money laundering.[^1] Dismantled by the Trump administration in 2025.[^2]

## Overview

OCDETF was the federal government's premier task-force mechanism for coordinating major drug-trafficking and transnational-organized-crime investigations across federal partners in Justice, Homeland Security, Treasury, Labor, State, and the U.S. Postal Service, as well as state and local law enforcement.[^3] Its institutional purpose per Senator Wyden's [February 2026 DEA letter](https://www.finance.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/letter_from_senator_wyden_to_dea_2-25-26.pdf) was to "identify, disrupt and dismantle major organized crime and drug trafficking operations" and to "conduct sophisticated investigations into transnational organized crime and money laundering."[^4]

Characteristic OCDETF targets per Wyden include "dangerous drug cartels, the Russian mafia and violent gangs moving fentanyl and weapons."[^5] The Chain Reaction record shows that an OCDETF lead product was prepared;[^6] the cited letter does not establish that Epstein's enterprise received a formal transnational-organized-crime classification.

OCDETF operated a Fusion Center (OFC) that generated investigative memoranda coordinating federal-agency reporting on particular targets.[^8] The May 18, 2015 OCDETF Fusion Center memorandum on Epstein and 14 other targets (Wyden calls them co-conspirators) — internal identifier OFC-TP-15-12392, SODOFC-15-12392; DOJ Bates EFTA00173953 — is the primary document Wyden's letter relies on.[^9]

**OCDETF dismantlement (2025):** Roll Call reported on December 18, 2025 that DOJ told Congress OCDETF would be “eliminated” and removed from the organizational chart.[^10] The Reagan-era mechanism for multi-agency drug-enforcement coordination is therefore no longer operational.[^11]

## Connection to Operation Chain Reaction

OCDETF's role in the Epstein investigation is described in [Wyden, Ron. Letter to Terrance C. Cole, Administrator, U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration. U.S. Senate Committee on Finance, February 25, 2026.](https://www.finance.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/letter_from_senator_wyden_to_dea_2-25-26.pdf) — documenting, in Wyden's characterization, an OCDETF investigation of Epstein and 14 others for drug trafficking and money laundering; the underlying 2015 Fusion Center target profile, provided "for lead purposes only," reports that DEA reporting ties the targets to "illegitimate wire transfers" tied to "illicit drug and/or prostitution activities occurring in the U.S. Virgin Islands and New York City."[^12]

 Chain Reaction is an additional federal investigative vector (alongside SDFL 2006–2008, FBI 2019/2020, bank suspicious-activity reporting by JPM/DB/BofA/BNY Mellon documented in [Raskin, Jamie. Letter to Robin Vince, Chief Executive Officer, Bank of New York Mellon. U.S. House Committee on the Judiciary, October 8, 2025.](https://democrats-judiciary.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/democrats-judiciary.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/2025-10-08.raskin-to-vince-bny-mellon-re-epstein.pdf), and Epstein-Belyakov contacts described in [Wyden, Ron. Letter to Leon D. Black. U.S. Senate Committee on Finance, March 20, 2026 (redacted).](https://www.finance.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/senator_wyden_letter_to_leon_black_redacted.pdf)).

# Covert-Action Privatization

Edition label: `2026-10`

**Definition:** The structural pattern, discussed in [Loch K. Johnson (1989)](https://doi.org/10.2307/2600495),[^1] by which executive-branch actors raise funds for covert action outside the government's appropriations process — through foreign-government solicitation and private-citizen donations — in order to evade statutory restrictions that apply to appropriated funds (most notably the [Hughes-Ryan](atlas-273f511a70ee65148b5de2dc0bfc90677a8dfef583ffe4ee71e10bacfc7b20c9.md) presidential-finding requirement and the [Boland Amendments](atlas-e19565a32ce0b3775b2ab13bc91c29a1c384c64c47dd81ee039091863d7a072c.md)' funding prohibitions).[^2]

## Overview

Privatization is structurally significant because Hughes-Ryan and the Boland Amendments used appropriations levers,[^3] while the Intelligence Oversight Act of 1980 imposed notification duties on activities by any department, agency, or entity of the United States.[^4] Raising funds outside appropriations could therefore evade some funding controls, but not remove the entire statutory architecture. Per Johnson 1989, Iran-Contra solicited foreign-government and private-citizen funds and routed activity through NSC staff rather than the CIA to evade parts of the oversight matrix.[^5]

## Historical significance

**Reagan-era documented instances** (per Johnson 1989, citing Inouye-Hamilton Committees Report 1987 pp. 85–100, 352–53; *NYT* 1987d:4):[^6]

- **Foreign-government solicitation** — Brunei (the misdirected $10 million wire transfer);[^7] South Africa;[^12] Saudi Arabia[^8] (the IAA-1991 required findings to specify whether any third party would take part).[^13]
- **Private-citizen solicitation** — wealthy donors solicited by <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Oliver North</span> and the National Endowment for the Preservation of Liberty (NEPL).[^9]
- **Private-group covert-fundraising** — Civilian Material Assistance (Alabama), described by Johnson as "a spinoff of a network nurtured by the Reagan Administration" and one of the private groups that "have taken it upon themselves to raise funds for anti-communist guerrillas like the contras outside U.S. government channels but with the knowledge and encouragement of the President and the NSC staff."[^10]

**The 1990 Bush 41 veto on the "request" language.** The 1990 Senate Bill 2834 (precursor to the [IAA-1991](atlas-01ddbdf46d2d12ac962e38e57c964bec9fb88d63ec48c12d04fd0604805944e3.md)) included language defining U.S. government requests to third parties to undertake covert actions as themselves covert actions.[^14] Bush 41 vetoed the bill over this language, citing chilling effects on diplomatic exchanges.[^15] The intelligence committees removed the language; the IAA-1991 passed in modified form (per Haas 2023).[^16] The enacted 1991 law omitted the explicit third-party-request language but retained presidential findings, congressional reporting, third-party-participation disclosure and legality requirements.[^11]

## Open Questions

- Is there a causal connection between Reagan-era covert-action privatization and post-9/11 intelligence contracting, and what evidence could establish it?
- Has any post-1991 covert action been traced to non-appropriated funding of the kind Johnson documented, and what findings, notifications and third-party disclosures accompanied it?
- How widely was the Brunei $10 million wire-transfer misdirection reported at the time?
- What evidence would establish contractor-channeled financing, as distinct from foreign-government or private-citizen solicitation, as a general mode of covert-action privatization?
- Given that the contractor model channels appropriated funds through procurement contracts, is that model structurally comparable to Iran-Contra’s use of third-party donations and arms-sale proceeds?
- Are contractor procurement and third-party solicitation analytically distinct categories, or evolutionary stages of the same pattern?

# SAR Failure

Edition label: `2026-10`

**Definition:** The pattern of inadequate, late, or absent **[Suspicious Activity Report](atlas-50ced1a06bfa4c7969afbb7aac40c27be2d91cf2d5431ddb111ab0411cc33e08.md)** filings by U.S. financial institutions during their active relationship with <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Jeffrey Epstein</span> and his counterparties[^1] — measured against the **[Bank Secrecy Act](atlas-27921b10cb3cdde5dc90a6846d7d74b734dcb0c276d434b5ccbfd41bab9a4381.md)**'s "knows, suspects, or has reason to suspect" filing standard[^2] and 30-day filing deadline[^3]. At JPMorgan Chase, flagged activity totaled $4.3M across 2002–2016, against $1.3B flagged retroactively in August–September 2019 (post-arrest).[^4]

## Overview

"SAR failure" is an analytic label for a recurring bank-compliance pattern documented in the Epstein record.[^1] The pattern has two diagnostic features:

1. **Pre-arrest under-filing.** During the active client relationship, the bank files few SARs despite contemporaneous internal red flags[^5] (cash-structuring patterns[^6], Butterfly-Trust beneficiary distributions[^7], ATTORNEY-1 cash-withdrawal patterns[^8], etc.). The internal-knowledge record — drawn from unsealed civil-litigation discovery (JPMorgan Chase), a civil complaint (Bank of America), and a New York State Department of Financial Services consent order (Deutsche Bank) — shows the institution had access to facts that meet the BSA's filing standard.
2. **Post-arrest retroactive surge.** After Epstein's July 2019 arrest, the institution retroactively reports a disproportionate value and number of suspicious transactions — sometimes orders of magnitude more than during the active-relationship period.[^9] A Senate Finance Committee staff memorandum to Ranking Member Wyden treated the post-arrest surge as evidence supporting its concern that pre-arrest reporting was inadequate; the memorandum does not quote JPMorgan admitting inadequacy.[^10]

The asymmetry between the two is the diagnostic marker.
